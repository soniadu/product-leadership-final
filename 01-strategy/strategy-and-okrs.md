# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Project managers, finance teams and the field teams populate related data in the same place for a single source of truth so no information is fragmented and lost. |
| **Where to play** | Large construction enterprises that need to receive updates from their frontline teams for their complex analysis and planning. |
| **How to win** | A connected system for multi-site coordination and complex project management that is fed from field data without need for fragmented workflows and apps. |
| **Capabilities required** | Simple mobile capabilities with camera and chat/comment capabilities, ability to access chats and images to tie updates to complex project data. Free or low cost addition |
| **Management systems** | Reduction in churn and monthly number of chats and images sent on mobile in app |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not chase lower and lower mid-market customers, as the construction industry is complicated and slow for our sales-led company, and retaining high spend customers is more worth the effort

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Become the system of record for the upper mid market and large market customers.
>
> - **KR1:** Churn is down by 14% (10,000/70,000) in 6 months after feature launch. Assumptions: $5 - $50M annual revenue represents 70,000 customers. $25M to $50M annual revenue represents roughly 10,000 firms (upper mid-market). $50M+ annually represents 7000 companies and $100M+ annually represents 4000 companies. Overall it's about 91,000 customers, with 70,000 customers at risk of churning. Meridian may be able to save the 10,000 that are high mid-market.
> - **KR2:** Number of photos and comments made in the mobile feature increases by 50% in 3 months after feature launch. Assumptions: WhatsApp is free but fragmented, habits were formed. It takes time for new habits to form and/or organizations to rollout new workflows and policies
> - **KR3:** Time it takes enterprise customers to obtain needed information and associate it to the appropriate project within the platform is reduced by 83%. Assumptions: 30min to upload pictures and comments from separate app to within the platform for one project. If information and photos are already on the platform, then may take 5min to associate that data.

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** 1) "Your biggest assumption is that churn is caused by the field gap." We're not completely certain why the churn is happening, or why customers are choosing simpler if the enterprise users still find it useful even if the frontline workers are not. This pushes us towards a new build that is hard to reverser and can take long to implement. 2) "Not chasing lower and lower mid-market customers means you accept losing most of the churn you’re trying to solve." These customers while not as willing to pay more for the platform, does include a majority of the customer count. The original point is harsh and seems like we're no longer serving a subset of customers.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** Change: Improve the focus on mid market customers, as it seems like larger companies are stickier (thus safer from churn) yet slow to adopt and implement while smaller companies make a comparable revenue and are faster to adopt Defend: Ensure the addition is a thin layer on top of the platform as an MVP to quickly tackle all levels of customers.
